EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Local Effort Cooperative proposes a locally rooted, wellness-oriented cafe for
The Marsh: excellent coffee and pastry in the morning; nourishing, approachable
lunches; a well-stocked grab-and-go case throughout the day; and catering that
supports The Marsh's meetings, programs, celebrations, and member community.

The concept is designed for The Marsh's more than 9,000 active members and
approximately 700 daily visitors, with particular attention to older active
adults. The menu will emphasize vegetables, whole grains, satisfying protein,
clear dietary labeling, familiar food prepared carefully, and several price
points. Local Effort's Minnesota- and Midwest-first sourcing and worker-owned
structure bring a distinctive community benefit without making the food feel
exclusive or overly formal.

Local Effort has operated in the Twin Cities since 2022. Our current work spans
recurring weekly meal service, private dinners and events, office catering, and
wholesale food production for cafe partners. The Marsh cafe would bring those
capabilities together in one place: daily hospitality, prepared food for later,
and event service supported by the same kitchen and sourcing system.

2. OPERATIONAL PLAN

Proposed cafe concept: Local Effort at The Marsh

The cafe will be an approachable neighborhood counter inside a wellness and
recreation facility, not a special-occasion restaurant placed in the wrong
setting. The operating model has four connected parts:

1. Morning cafe: coffee, espresso, tea, smoothies, baked goods, yogurt and
   granola, and a warm breakfast item.
2. Lunch counter: soups, salads, grain bowls, sandwiches, pizza, and rotating
   seasonal plates.
3. Grab-and-go: clearly labeled meals, snacks, fruit, sandwiches, salads, and
   beverages available beyond staffed counter hours.
4. Catering and programming: meeting food, boxed lunches, facility events,
   member gatherings, tastings, and chef-led programs.

The menu will favor Minnesota and Midwestern ingredients where season, quality,
and supply allow. We will maintain vegetarian, vegan, gluten-aware, and
high-protein choices and label common allergens. Familiarity and ease matter:
the menu will always include simple choices for a guest who wants coffee and a
pastry, soup and bread, a sandwich, or a balanced meal after exercise.

Staffing approach:

- One founder-level operating lead accountable for the City relationship,
  financial performance, food quality, and daily standards.
- A kitchen and pastry lead responsible for production planning, sanitation,
  purchasing, and opening/closing controls.
- Cross-trained cooks, counter staff, and baristas scheduled to daypart demand.
- Flexible event staff added for catering and programmed activity rather than
  burdening normal cafe shifts with unnecessary fixed labor.
- Written opening, closing, temperature, allergen, cleaning, cash-control, and
  incident procedures.
- Training in food safety, hospitality, accessibility, dietary communication,
  and the needs of older active adults.

The initial schedule is expected to require one kitchen lead, one production
cook or baker, and one counter/barista role during core hours, with founder
coverage and peak/event support. Final headcount and shift design will follow a
site walk-through, equipment test, and forecast by daypart.

Customer service philosophy:

Hospitality should be warm, direct, and useful. A guest should be greeted,
understand what is available, know what a dish costs, and receive a clear answer
about dietary needs without feeling rushed or talked down to. We will learn
regulars' preferences, invite feedback, resolve problems at the counter when
possible, and coordinate closely with Marsh staff when a facility issue affects
the guest experience. Good service also means consistency: posted hours, a
dependable core menu, accurate labels, clean spaces, and fast communication.

Special programming:

Potential programs include seasonal grower tastings, practical nutrition and
cooking demonstrations, soup or bread workshops, member coffee hours, youth
food activities, post-workout meal features, and menus tied to Marsh events.
Beer and wine service may be added only after the parties agree on its role and
all licensing, insurance, storage, staffing, and service requirements are met.


3. FINANCIAL PROPOSAL

Proposed percentage rent:

- 12 percent of onsite sales
- 4 percent of offsite and internet sales

Local Effort proposes this percentage-rent structure in place of fixed base
rent. For lease drafting, we propose that sales taxes, gratuities paid to staff,
refunds, discounts, and complimentary items be excluded from the percentage-
rent calculation. "Onsite" would mean sales made to guests at The Marsh,
including cafe counter and onsite event sales. "Offsite and internet" would
mean orders generated through external or online channels and produced or
fulfilled through the Marsh operation. The City's lease form will control the
final definitions, reporting rights, due dates, audit provisions, utilities,
and any exclusions.

Local Effort will provide monthly point-of-sale reporting and remit percentage
rent on an agreed monthly schedule. The proposed structure aligns the City's
return with actual sales while preserving working capital during the opening
ramp.

Financial capability:

Local Budget's current posted, split-aware operating report shows approximately
$131,800 of net operating revenue for the twelve months ending July 31, 2026.
The same report shows approximately $36,900 of cash-basis operating income
before founder draws, founder compensation, and unclassified spending. After
reserving the full $1,900 of currently unclassified spending for that period,
the corresponding cash operating result is approximately $35,000 before founder
draws and founder compensation. These are management cash figures, not audited
financial statements and not a claim of fully loaded profit. They may change as
pending bank activity posts or reviewed classifications are updated.

The financial case for The Marsh does not rely on a single new revenue stream.
Daily cafe sales, grab-and-go, catering, recurring prepared meals, and offsite
or internet orders can share kitchen production, purchasing, and labor. Local
Effort's existing partner-wholesale records demonstrate repeat production and
reporting, including observed periods of 88-297 units per week.

Before lease execution, Local Effort will provide the City, under appropriate
confidentiality, with an opening budget, evidence of the committed startup

- receiving and loading-dock access, waste, grease, recycling, and compost;
- electrical capacity, internet, POS connectivity, office access, and security;
- dining-room furniture, counter fixtures, menu boards, and exterior signage;
- accessibility and required health, building, fire, liquor, and safety approvals.

Local Effort expects to provide its POS system, portable smallwares, opening
inventory, packaging, and business-specific tools. This proposal assumes the
City delivers the existing premises and fixed equipment in safe, licensed,
serviceable condition and provides reasonable pre-opening access. The lease
should assign utilities, repairs, preventative maintenance, capital replacement,
janitorial boundaries, pest control, waste service, and after-hours access
explicitly.


6. BENEFIT TO THE MARSH AND THE COMMUNITY

Local Effort offers The Marsh a cafe that is specific to the facility rather
than a generic concession operation. The concept supports wellness without
turning food into a lecture; supports older active adults without excluding
families or youth; creates useful choices at several price points; and extends
The Marsh's event capability. The cooperative model also gives cafe workers a
path to ownership and a voice in the decisions that affect their work.

The result should be a dependable everyday amenity and a reason to spend more
time at The Marsh: coffee with a friend, lunch after a class, a prepared meal to
take home, or food for a meeting or celebration.
