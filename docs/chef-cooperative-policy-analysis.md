# Chef cooperative — dues, ownership, and operating policy

Owner: Local Effort Cooperative
As of: 2026-10-15
Status: Design recommendations for owner/member/counsel review, not adopted terms, an equity offering, or observed financial results.
Review by: Before admitting working members, collecting dues, promising ownership, or publishing final agreements.

Source brief: [owner proposal](./chef-cooperative-structure-proposal.md). This analysis adds recommendations; it does not rewrite existing ownership, capital accounts, Localist membership, or governing documents. The website may recruit applicants, but applying does not constitute admission, employment, a paid-work contract, or ownership.

## Recommendation in brief

- Model **$50 per month in operating dues**, starting only after approved admission and explicit acceptance of final terms. **No fees due at signup.** Do not collect dues while the terms remain proposed. Avoid annual prepayment until the pilot proves its benefits and cancellation/refund policy.
- Keep operating dues, member capital, compensation, and patronage in separate accounts and agreements. Dues buy cooperative services, not a percentage of the company. Do not turn hours into an unfunded fixed-dollar equity promise.
- Prefer a labor-based ownership path with individual capital accounts and democratic membership rights, subject to counsel reconciling the current capitalization and membership classes. Allow an alternative sustained-participation path so scarce available work does not make ownership inaccessible. Dues alone should not purchase extra votes or preferential job allocation.
- Launch the applicant/profile/review process now; final membership admission, work access, payroll invitations, tax collection, public directory publication, billing, and equity allocations each require their own evidence and approvals. Never grant staff authorization because someone applied.

At twelve members, $50 dues nearly funds the modeled operation but misses the full reserve-inclusive target by $473.28 a year. The owner must explicitly fund that gap or reduce the approved budget; do not assume referrals will cover it. With every member paying, break-even is twelve, but planning on perfect collection hides the access commitment. At six members a pilot needs about $270/month of disclosed support. At eighteen members the projected margin is not distributable profit: actual costs, taxes, liabilities and required reserves come first.

**Why $50 rather than $25 or $75:** $25 requires a scale beyond a small founding cohort; $75 works with fewer members but asks chefs to carry a high access cost before referrals/kitchen capacity are proven. $50 is a testable middle case, not a discovered market price. Run a three-month pilot with a member-approved service budget and member feedback before adopting it permanently. Do not charge for priority work without actually offering the agreed service.

### Holding and spending dues

Recommended controls, to be adopted in the agreements and accounting policy:

- Dues belong to the cooperative as service revenue, not to an individual savings wallet or an escrow account. The accountant determines recognition timing, taxability, and deferred-revenue treatment; never describe ordinary dues as refundable equity.
- Use a separately identifiable operating ledger/cost center and reserve ledger; a separate bank subaccount can help cash discipline but does not itself create a legal trust. The modeled reserve target is $1,500, three months of the full $500 funding target; accumulated reserves remain cooperative funds, not personal balances.
- Approve an annual budget with working-member oversight; report assessed/collected/waived dues, expenses, reserve cash, and commitments quarterly. Require disinterested approval for related-party spending and preserve invoices.
- Recommend monthly billing in advance only after admission, a clear cancellation cutoff, no unapproved auto-renewal, prorated refund of unused prepaid service if the cooperative ends access, and a documented dispute process. Counsel must settle actual notice/refund rules.
- Offer confidential hardship reductions or waivers. A waiver should count as good standing and should not remove an otherwise admitted member's vote or labor patronage. Do not promise unlimited waivers without funding them in the budget.
- Kitchen hours, referral economics, job compensation and any barter are individually written and priced. Never silently offset dues against earned pay. Prep-for-kitchen exchanges require fair valuation, tax treatment, safety review and an agreement; they are not free labor.

## Ownership accumulation: recommended structure

### Separate four things that are easy to confuse

1. **Operating dues** fund services and are not a share purchase.
2. **Membership admission/voting** follows an approved membership class, agreement, and governance process. Recommend one working member, one vote within that class; resolve how the class relates to existing owners, customer Localists and capital members before promising control.
3. **Contributed member capital** is actual money or a legally accepted, valued contribution, recorded separately from operating dues. A possible $250 initial capital requirement, payable over time, is an internal design option only—not a fee currently due, an approved security, or a public offer. If adopted, disclose it in addition to dues; never hide it in the $50 model. Waivers/installments must not become wage deductions without lawful consent.
4. **Retained patronage capital** is an allocation of actual allocable cooperative surplus, not wages, tips, a fixed return, an account holding cash, or a perpetual percentage of enterprise value.

### Eligibility recommendation, not an automated grant

After orientation and **at least 90 days** of participation, recommend eligibility for admission/ownership review through either:

- **Work path:** 40 verified, compensated cooperative work hours (or separately authorized qualifying contribution hours) plus orientation and agreement completion; or
- **Sustained-participation path:** six months in good standing under paid or expressly waived dues, plus 12 verified governance/education/contribution hours. Paying cash alone does not buy labor patronage or control; define qualifying contributions and lawful compensation with counsel.

These are proposed gates for review, not promises of immediate ownership or reasons to withhold ordinary pay. Do not require unpaid productive work as a condition of joining. Paid time remains payable whether or not a chef becomes a member. Validate whether the owner wants hours **or** dues alone to trigger eligibility; the recommendation adds a contribution requirement to the dues path to keep working-member ownership tied to participation. The website must not silently present this recommendation as adopted owner instruction.

Admission should be decided against published objective criteria by the authorized body, with recorded reasons and an appeal route; it is not an unchecked owner's veto. Prevent self-approval of hours and conflicts of interest. Keep pre-admission contributions separately identified; counsel/accountant must decide whether any may count after admission. No retroactive claims on past profits or existing owners' interests are implied.

### Accrual after admission

Recommend verified labor hours as the transparent patronage measure (one eligible hour, one unit) rather than pay rate, dues amount, popularity, or client revenue. Record paid production, paid prep and approved governance/education separately; decide which categories qualify in the adopted policy. Independent jobs merely referred to a chef do not automatically count as cooperative patronage. Kitchen barter must not count twice. A correction/reversal should reference the original approved record rather than rewriting history.

After period close, accountant review, debt/solvency checks, and approval of the **working-member allocable surplus pool** under the governing documents:

`member allocation = approved working-member allocable surplus × member eligible hours ÷ total eligible member hours`

Illustration only: a $10,000 approved pool, 2,400 eligible hours, and a member with 120 hours yields a $500 allocation. If the adopted policy retained 50%, $250 would enter that member's capital account and $250 would be payable in cash. This is not $250 of cash held for the member, a fixed company percentage, or a promise of a return. The model deliberately does not derive the $10,000 from total company profit: existing class rights and 308B allocations must be resolved first. Zero allocable surplus yields no new patronage equity; hours are not a dollar liability. Loss allocation, collective reserves, tax treatment and qualified/nonqualified notices require accountant/counsel decisions before this formula becomes operative.

Recommend no forfeiture of already allocated, vested capital merely for leaving; permit only documented losses/offsets and lawful agreed terms. No speculative appreciation, secondary transfer or guaranteed repurchase. A draft redemption approach is a review after departure with installments subject to solvency and the approved agreement—not immediate withdrawal or a guaranteed date. Counsel must set priority, any waiting period, death/disability handling, dilution, losses, disputes, and statutory constraints. Show book capital separately from voting status and cash actually payable.

### Why not a percent per hour or per dues payment?

A fixed 0.01% per hour eventually exceeds available ownership, dilutes existing holders without an authorized issuance, rewards hours even when the cooperative loses money, and creates valuation/tax issues. Cash-weighted votes undermine the working-member rationale. A capital-account model connects economic accumulation to earned surplus while allowing equal class votes; it still needs a valid instrument and governing documents. A different model may be adopted after professional review, but do not implement either by incrementing a user-profile percentage.

| Payroll/tax | Owner-approved secure Square onboarding and verified contractor classification | Never collect W-9 in public bios; no automated Payroll invite in this build |
| Ownership period close | Approved contribution history, class rights, reviewed surplus and allocation approval | No live equity issuance or invented current cap table |

Referral/prep allocation recommendation: publish suitability and food-safety requirements, offer to eligible available members first, use a rotating opportunity order among equally suitable members, give an explicit response deadline, retain client choice, and record declined/expired assignments. Dues level, ownership balance and personal connections should not purchase queue priority. Kitchen use remains a separately negotiated schedule with capacity/cancellation/insurance terms. These operational policies require approval before a live work marketplace; a profile is not an available job pool.

Keep W-9 documents and legal tax identifiers outside ordinary profile storage, analytics and logs.

## Sources and limits

Primary/current pages accessed 2026-10-15; verify subsequent amendments with counsel. These sources support the concepts, not the proposed dollar amounts or thresholds:

- [Minnesota 308B.545 — member voting](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/308B.545): patron member voting and class voting are governed by statute and the actual articles/bylaws. Do not assume a marketing label determines control.
- [Minnesota 308B.721 — allocations and distributions](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/308B.721): allocation between patron and other interests depends on the governing documents and statutory limits. This memo does not elect a different class split.
- [US Federation of Worker Cooperatives — worker-cooperative definition](https://www.usworker.coop/what-is-a-worker-cooperative/): labor-based economic participation, democratic representation and worker control. A referral/services cooperative of independent businesses may need a different legal description than an employee worker cooperative; resolve structure, do not infer it from the title.
- [ICA Group — Internal Capital Accounts](https://institute.coop/sites/default/files/resources/Internal-Capital-Accounts.pdf): individual/collective accounts, labor patronage, retained allocations, losses and redemption. Educational model, not current tax advice.
- [Square — run payroll for contractors](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6006-pay-contractors-through-square-payroll): product workflow, not worker-classification advice or proof this account is configured.
