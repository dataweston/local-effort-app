## Comparison with the standing estimate

The prior 2026-07-23 recommendation used a roughly **$50,000** staged raise
case built around three hires (junior 15 hours at $25/hour, a second chef 15
hours at $35/hour, and sales/commission plus customer-service help), Maria at
15-20 hours, packaging/logistics, and throughput. It was a model, not an
approved allocation.

The owner list is close in its recognition that capacity, packaging, and
commercial follow-through are the constraints, but it is broader and less
priced:

- It removes Maria's regular labor from the baseline, correctly replacing it
  with occasional-job flexibility.
- It replaces the earlier two-part-time-kitchen assumption with one full-time
  owner-worker and two part-time kitchen workers. This may better express the
  desired capacity, but it increases the missing labor budget materially.
- It gives explicit weight to inventory, cashflow recovery, marketing,
  equipment, packaging, and technology. The earlier estimate did not price
  these as a complete owner list.
- It adds the sales/customer-service role at a defined wage and schedule, but
  commission and employer burden remain open.
- It does not yet price the equipment and packaging that determine whether the
  planned labor can actually produce and fulfill at higher volume.

## Fused planning view

### $65,000 initial case

Treat the $43,350 known subtotal as the owner-prioritized core, then use the
remaining $21,650 to price and stage the minimum viable equipment, packaging,
sales/customer-service base wage and commission, kitchen staffing, and reserve.
Do not represent the full kitchen staffing plan as funded until its rates,
hours, payroll burden, and facility impact are modeled. The payroll reserve
must be explicitly split so the sales hire is not counted twice or assumed to
cover kitchen labor that it cannot cover.

### $120,000 high case

The high case adds **$55,000** of planning capacity beyond the initial ask. Use
that increment first to fully cost the one-full-time/two-part-time kitchen
build, essential production and packaging equipment, and a real working-capital
reserve. Only then stage additional hires and the front-of-house team. Keep
the event director, visual director, packaging manager, and human
customer-service/sales agents as gated expansion rather than automatic use of
funds.
