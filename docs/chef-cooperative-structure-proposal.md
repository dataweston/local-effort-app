# Chef Cooperative Structure — Owner Proposal

Owner: Local Effort Cooperative
As of: 2026-10-15
Status: Owner-proposed design input; not adopted by a board and not legal, tax, labor, or securities advice.
Review by: Before board formation, bylaws/articles drafting, or public signup/benefit promises.

This document records the owner's current proposed direction for a chef-member cooperative path. It is additive to existing cooperative, capitalization, staffing, and Localist materials; it does not retroactively amend those records. Where older descriptions conflict with this specific path, use the owner's current instruction here as the working design input, while retaining prior statements as historical evidence until reviewed and reconciled. Nothing here establishes that the relevant membership, equity, governance, contractor, payroll, or securities terms have been legally approved.

## Proposed model

Build a team of independent chefs, including working chefs who want to become independent. For this path, chefs join as dues-paying cooperative members; they are not hired into employment by Local Effort. Working members may become eligible for an ownership stake. Separate any other employee, contractor, or worker relationship from this member pathway rather than silently treating the categories as interchangeable.

## Proposed working-member rights and benefits

These are design goals to work through before governance documents or public promises—not finalized entitlements or guaranteed work:

- **Referrals and job opportunities:** member chefs receive preferred status for opportunities Local Effort receives: first opportunity to claim/accept suitable jobs, or a recommendation to clients to hire a cooperative member. Define eligibility, ordering, response windows, conflicts, client choice, and quality/safety requirements.
- **Prep-hour pool:** available prep hours are pooled and made available to members first. This is priority access to available work, not a guarantee of hours or earnings. Define how hours are posted, claimed, assigned, paid, and reconciled.
- **Kitchen access:** members have a right to seek/negotiate kitchen hours, not automatically allotted hours. Hours are negotiated individually in light of the member's needs and contributions; prep work may, for example, be exchanged for kitchen hours if mutually agreed. Define scheduling, capacity, permitted use, cost allocation, safety, insurance, and cancellation rules.
- **Promotion:** members may receive promotional space on the website and in other physical spaces. Define the opt-in, content, eligibility, standards, and maintenance/removal process.
- **Ownership eligibility/accrual:** members become eligible to accumulate an ownership stake after a still-to-be-defined threshold based on working/contributing a certain number of hours, paying dues, or a combination. Define the qualifying contribution, accrual formula, start date, vesting, classes, voting/economic rights, dilution, transfer/redemption, exit, and treatment of inactive members. Do not imply an automatic share or fixed percentage until these are decided and approved.

## Dues and onboarding direction

- Present dues amount and terms up front on the website signup page, but allow signup without dues payment: **no fees due at signup**.
- The dues amount, due date, collection method, accounting treatment, ownership/purpose of funds, permitted spending, reserve/refund rules, and member oversight are undecided. Do not infer or advertise a price or spending promise.
- Signup should lead to an authenticated, gated Hub view designed for working members.
- Decide which minimal eligibility, consent, and work-preference questions belong in signup, versus the fuller member profile built after enrollment. Avoid collecting sensitive tax/payment data before a secure, justified workflow exists.
- The full work profile is intended to include W-9 information and other work-related information. A separate optional public profile may include biography, photos, and services offered. Keep tax/work records private; public-profile fields must be explicitly opt-in and separately controlled.

## Operations and payment direction

The owner proposes paying member chefs as contractors using Square Payroll/Teams. Treat this as a requested operational direction, not a conclusion about worker classification or a statement that the Square products support the intended workflow. Before implementation, verify current Square product capability and have employment/cooperative counsel and the accountant review classification, tax reporting, member compensation, and the interaction between cooperative membership and paid services. Never collect or store W-9 data in an ordinary public profile or unprotected signup flow.

## Decisions required before board and articles/bylaws

1. Membership classes and admission/termination, dues amount, timing, waivers, use/restrictions, reserves, refundability, and member approval/oversight.
2. Governance rights: voting basis, member meeting/board roles, elections, reserved matters, delegation, quorum, and dispute/appeal process.
3. Relationship between consumer Localists, chef working members, existing owners, and any investors/capital contributors; eligibility and whether each class has votes, patronage, distributions, or no such rights.
4. Worker-member ownership mechanism and thresholds, vesting/accrual, instruments/classes, economic and voting rights, valuation, dilution, transfer/redemption, exit, and funding source. Reconcile against existing owner cap-table and capitalization records rather than overwriting them.
5. Referral/job allocation, prep pool, kitchen-access negotiation, client choice, insurance, food safety, equipment and facility costs, cancellations, and accountability.
6. Compensation model and Square workflow, including contractor classification and tax reporting review.
7. Signup and Hub access lifecycle, profile/privacy controls, public listing consent, data retention/security, and opt-out/removal.
8. Review proposed rights and obligations with Minnesota cooperative, employment/tax, and securities counsel and the accountant; then map approved decisions into articles/bylaws, member agreements, policies, and public copy.

## Pre-board sequencing

Use this proposal as a question set and design brief before board formation and articles/bylaws drafting. First settle the member classes, rights, governance, ownership path, dues economics, and paid-work relationship; then obtain professional review and convert approved choices into governing documents. Do not treat the website signup, public marketing copy, or a Brain capture as legal adoption of these terms.
